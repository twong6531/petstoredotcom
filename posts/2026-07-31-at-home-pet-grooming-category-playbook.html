Professional Grooming Costs $900 a Year. Here's the $14.8 Billion At-Home Grooming Category Every Online Pet Store Should Be Building.

📅 July 31, 2026 ✍️ PetStore.com Editorial Team 🏷️ Strategy · Grooming · Category Building

A full groom for a Golden Retriever runs $80–95 per appointment in 2026, and Goldens need grooming six to eight times a year. That's up to $760 annually — before tips, before gas, before waiting an hour for pickup. For a growing cohort of cost-conscious pet owners, the math has finally broken against the professional salon. Industry reports now list DIY grooming as the #1 threat cited by professional grooming businesses. The at-home pet grooming products market is worth $14.8 billion in 2026 and heading to $28.6 billion by 2035. That shift is your opportunity.

The Market Shift Professional Groomers Are Losing Sleep Over

The global pet grooming market sits at $19.5 billion in 2026, growing at 9.1% CAGR toward $46.7 billion by 2036. But the split inside that number is what matters for online retailers. Professional salon services are growing — but at-home grooming products are growing faster, and e-commerce is the fastest-growing distribution channel in the category, outpacing specialty pet retail and veterinary distributors.

According to 2026 market analysis, DIY grooming solutions are the primary headwind cited by professional grooming businesses, with cost-conscious pet owners supplementing or replacing salon visits with quality at-home tools. Pet parents who went monthly to a groomer are now buying a $45 slicker brush, a $28 deshedding tool, and an $18 bottle of professional-grade shampoo — and handling maintenance themselves between less-frequent professional appointments.

For your online store, this is textbook category disruption moving in your direction. The margins reinforce the case: 40–65% on grooming tools, 50–70% on shampoos and conditioners. This is not a category to treat as filler.

The Four Pillars of a Profitable At-Home Grooming Category

A well-built grooming category isn't a random assortment of brushes. It has four distinct pillars, each serving a different need and price point.

Deshedding and Coat Tools — The anchor product. A quality deshedding brush or undercoat rake is the first purchase a new at-home groomer makes and the product they reorder when it eventually wears out. FURminator (Spectrum Brands) dominates name recognition with stainless-steel deshedding tools that retail for $25–60 and move fast. Self-cleaning slicker brushes and double-sided undercoat rakes are strong cross-sells. Average customer spend in this segment: $40–80 per order.

Grooming-Grade Shampoos and Conditioners — Your consumable anchor and your autoship play. Earthbath's oatmeal and aloe shampoo is a perennial bestseller at $14–18 per bottle. 4-Legger, USDA-certified organic, commands $18–22 and converts well to customers who scrutinize ingredient labels. Natural and breed-specific formulas are the fastest-growing sub-segment. Reorder frequency: every 2–3 months like clockwork.

Clippers, Trimmers, and Finishing Tools — The highest-ticket pillar. Professional-grade cordless clippers from Andis, Wahl, and Oneisall retail for $50–150 and carry 45–55% margins when sourced through pet wholesale. These are deliberate, researched purchases — solid product pages and comparison guides drive conversions. One clipper sale often comes with $60+ in blade and attachment add-ons.

Ear Care, Paw Care, and Specialty Products — The upsell pillar. Pet wipes, ear cleaning solutions, paw balms, and dental rinses average $12–22 each and get added to grooming-focused carts without much selling. Veterinarian's Best, Zymox, and Musher's Secret carry genuine consumer loyalty in this space. These round out orders and often tip a cart over your free-shipping threshold.

Brands Worth Stocking (and the Margins They Carry)

Three tiers have clear footholds in the at-home grooming market:

FURminator (Spectrum Brands) — The category anchor. Customers search for it specifically, which means stocking it pulls branded search traffic to your store. Margins are tighter (30–35%) but the halo effect lifts adjacent products across the category.

Earthbath — The natural/biodegradable shampoo leader. Broad distribution but not commodity-priced. Its customer base buys on ingredient quality rather than price — loyal, low churn, perfect autoship material.

4-Legger — USDA-certified organic shampoos at a premium price point. Converts well to the natural-pet-parent segment already buying premium food. The brand storytelling is strong and does some of the selling for you.

Andis / Wahl — The two pro-grade clipper brands indie stores should prioritize. Both carry credibility signals from actual professional groomers, which makes them credible to aspiring DIY groomers. Andis EPOCH cordless clippers retail around $120–140; Wahl ARCO runs $85–100. Both carry 45%+ margins through pet wholesale channels.

Burt's Bees for Pets — Mass-recognition natural positioning at an accessible price point. A strong entry-level shampoo that introduces customers to your grooming category before they graduate to Earthbath or 4-Legger.

The Bundle Strategy That Lifts AOV by 30–35%

Grooming converts exceptionally well as a bundled category because the customer need is obvious, the product pairings are intuitive, and there's no brand loyalty battle inside the bundle. Three proven structures:

The Starter Kit Bundle ($55–75): Slicker brush + deshedding tool + 1 bottle of shampoo + paw wipes. Target: first-time dog owners, recent adopters, new rescue parents. Price it at 10–15% off the individual sum and position it as the "everything you need to groom at home" setup kit. Add-to-cart rates on bundles like this run 20–30% higher than individual product pages.

The Seasonal Shedding Bundle ($45–65): Undercoat rake + deshedding tool + shed-control shampoo. Target: double-coated breed owners in late summer — right now. Late July through August is peak coat-blowing season for Huskies, Goldens, German Shepherds, and Bernese Mountain Dogs. A targeted email to known double-coat owners this week will perform.

The Long-Coat Breed Bundle ($70–95): High-quality slicker brush + detangling spray + finishing comb + conditioning shampoo. Target: owners of Goldens, Collies, Doodles, and similar breeds. This segment is underserved by the big retailers and will pay a premium for breed-specific curation.

Why Grooming Is Your Most Natural Autoship Category

Here's what makes grooming products uniquely powerful for building recurring revenue: shampoos, conditioners, ear cleaners, paw wipes, and dental rinses are all consumables with predictable reorder windows. There's no guesswork about when a customer will run out.

A 16-oz bottle of Earthbath shampoo lasts a medium-sized dog about 2 months. A 32-oz professional jug lasts 4–5 months. The reorder trigger is perfectly predictable — and perfectly suited for autoship. Run the math: a customer who puts their grooming shampoo on a 10-week autoship cadence and adds ear cleaner and paw wipes at the same frequency is generating $35–45 every 2.5 months automatically. That's $168–216 per year from a single customer, at a customer acquisition cost of zero after the first conversion.

A "Grooming Essentials Autoship" — shampoo + conditioner + one specialty product — with 10–15% off and free shipping is one of the fastest autoship wins available in any category. The need is frequent, the reorder date is predictable, and the "why subscribe?" conversation practically writes itself.

The Video Content That Sells Grooming Products

Grooming is a highly visual, anxiety-driven purchase. First-time at-home groomers are nervous — they want to see it done before they buy the tool. Short-form video demonstrating grooming techniques paired with product links drives measurable conversion. The highest-performing grooming content follows a consistent formula: breed-specific title ("How to deshed a Husky in 15 minutes") → real product demo → link to your store.

You don't need a film crew. A 60-second video of someone running a FURminator across a Golden and showing the pile of removed fur is consistently among the most shared pet content on TikTok and Instagram Reels. If your product is tagged in the video, that pile of fur is actively selling for you.

Three evergreen content pillars for grooming: "First time grooming at home" tutorials (anxiety-driven, high buyer intent), "Summer shedding survival" videos (seasonal, timely right now through September), and "Breed-specific grooming tips" (long-tail, evergreen, pulls organic search traffic for years).

The At-Home Grooming Category Quick-Start Checklist

If you're building this category from scratch, this is where to start — all of it completable inside one week:

  • Stock one anchor deshedding tool (FURminator or comparable) and one quality slicker brush
  • Add two shampoo lines: one natural/organic (Earthbath or 4-Legger) and one accessible brand (Burt's Bees for Pets)
  • Create one bundle product page — the Starter Kit — with a 10% bundle discount
  • Write one breed-specific grooming guide for your top three most-searched dog breeds
  • Add a "Subscribe & Save" option on every shampoo and conditioner product page
  • Shoot one 60-second grooming demo video for your best-selling product

Six tasks. One week. A category that compounds for years because the consumable reorder cycle is built into the product itself.

The Bottom Line

Your customers are already Googling "how to groom my dog at home." The at-home grooming products market is heading from $14.8 billion to $28.6 billion over the next decade. The only question is whether those searches land on your product pages or a competitor's.

Pet owners who groom at home buy tools, shampoos, and specialty products repeatedly, on a predictable cadence, and they're actively looking for a store they trust to guide them. That's what independent pet stores do better than anyone. The window to establish your grooming category before it becomes crowded is right now — and summer shedding season makes this week the perfect moment to act.
