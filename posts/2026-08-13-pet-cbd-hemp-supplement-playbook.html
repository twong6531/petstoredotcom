Pet CBD Is Growing 32% a Year and Your Customers Are Already Buying It — Just Not From You

📅 August 13, 2026 ✍️ PetStore.com Editorial Team 🏷️ Wellness · CBD · Supplements

The pet CBD market hit $576 million in 2026. By 2034, analysts project it will reach $5.47 billion — a 32.5% compound annual growth rate that makes it the fastest-growing segment in the entire pet supplement space. And yet walk into the average independent pet store right now and you'll find zero CBD products on the shelf.

Not because owners don't know about it. Because they're scared of it. The legal gray area, the FDA's position, the risk of saying something wrong — it's enough to make most indie retailers decide to wait and see. Here's what that waiting costs you: Charlotte's Web is already distributed to 6,000+ specialty pet stores through Phillips Pet Food & Supplies. Kradle landed in 1,400 Tractor Supply locations in 2025. Your customers are already buying CBD for their dogs and cats somewhere. The question is whether that somewhere is you.

Why the Repeat Purchase Rate Changes Everything

Pet CBD isn't a one-and-done category. It behaves like a recurring wellness protocol, not an impulse purchase. A dog on a daily CBD oil regimen works through a 30ml tincture in roughly 4–6 weeks. A cat on daily soft chews burns through a 60-count bag in 30–60 days.

The repeat purchase rate for pet CBD buyers is among the highest in the entire hemp category. And pet supplement buyers in general have checkout abandonment rates of just 51.29% — the lowest of any ecommerce vertical tracked by Klaviyo. These are committed, intentional shoppers. When they find a brand and a source they trust, they reorder automatically — long before you even set up an autoship flow.

The retailers seeing the most success with this category aren't the ones shouting CBD the loudest. They're the ones who stock only third-party tested products, train staff to explain what CBD can and cannot do, and build a simple reorder path for customers who find something that works. That's an indie store advantage, not a Chewy one.

The Regulatory Reality: What You Can and Cannot Say

Here's the honest legal picture as of August 2026: hemp-derived CBD with less than 0.3% THC is federally legal under the 2018 Farm Bill. The FDA has not approved CBD as a drug or treatment for animals — which means making therapeutic claims is prohibited without a New Animal Drug Application. In June 2025, the FDA issued a new round of warning letters to manufacturers and retailers using unapproved drug language.

In practice, the line is straightforward. You can use wellness language that doesn't make disease claims:

  • ✓ "Formulated to support calm behavior"
  • ✓ "Hemp-derived support for active seniors"
  • ✓ "Daily wellness chew with full-spectrum hemp extract"
  • ✗ "Treats separation anxiety"
  • ✗ "Reduces joint pain"
  • ✗ "Anti-inflammatory"

The American Association of Veterinary State Boards published a formal Guidance Document for CBD Use on Companion Animals in March 2026 — it's worth reading before you train your staff. Most states allow retail sales as long as products meet state hemp testing and labeling requirements, though several Northeast and West Coast states have added stricter mandates. Check your state agriculture department before your first order.

The Five Use Cases That Drive 90% of Pet CBD Sales

Understanding why customers buy lets you merchandise and staff-train smarter. Based on retail and DTC purchase data from the leading brands, five use cases account for nearly all pet CBD volume:

  1. Anxiety relief — Thunderstorms, fireworks, car rides, vet visits, and separation anxiety. This is the #1 driver by volume, and back-to-school season (right now, in your market) is a genuine sales spike as dogs struggle with the sudden quiet.
  2. Senior joint and mobility support — Older dogs and cats whose owners are looking for a gentler daily option. High repeat rate, high urgency, and pairs naturally with your existing glucosamine and omega-3 category.
  3. Post-procedure recovery — Surgery, dental cleanings, stressful vet visits. Often a first-time CBD purchase that converts to an ongoing buyer when it works.
  4. Skin and coat support — Frequently sold as a bundle with topical hemp balms and serums.
  5. General daily wellness — Owners who add CBD to their pet's regimen the same way they take a daily omega-3 or probiotic themselves.

Anchor your initial merchandising around anxiety and senior wellness. Those two use cases alone will justify the category — and they both have natural autoship cadences baked in.

The Top Wholesale Brands Worth Stocking

The pet CBD market has no shortage of brands you shouldn't touch. These are the ones that consistently clear indie retail vetting in 2026:

Pet Releaf — USDA Organic certified, Colorado-grown hemp, with a wholesale program that accepts opening orders as low as $100. Full third-party Certificates of Analysis for every production batch. This is the brand most serious indie retailers lead with when building the category.

HolistaPet — Founded by holistic veterinarians, with condition-specific formulations that pair CBD with complementary herbs: valerian root in the calm formulas, glucosamine in the joint support line. Clean FDA-compliant language throughout. Strong price-to-quality ratio at retail, which means customers come back without sticker shock.

Medterra Pets — More than 25,000 reviews across their product line, U.S. Hemp Authority certified, and they use CBD isolate (99.6% pure, zero THC). This is your answer for customers worried about THC showing up in drug tests or interacting with medications. Very clean label, easy consumer education story.

Innovet Pet — One of the longest-running pet CBD brands, with a flexible wholesale program, broad product range (tinctures, capsules, soft chews, topicals), and low minimum order quantities. A good choice for stores that want to test the category before committing to a large opening order.

Charlotte's Web — The category's most recognized name, now in 6,000+ stores via Phillips Pet Food & Supplies. If you're already a Phillips account, adding Charlotte's Web requires a single conversation. Brand recognition alone drives walk-in traffic.

Pricing, Margins, and What the Shelf Looks Like

The economics are favorable. Pet CBD tinctures retail between $25 and $80 depending on potency and brand. Soft chew bags run $25–$60. Wholesale margins on pet CBD typically land at 45–55% — significantly better than conventional pet food (usually 20–30%) and on par with your premium supplement SKUs.

Pet owners consistently pay more for CBD pet products than they would for comparable human wellness items. A 2026 Grand View Research analysis confirmed that willingness to pay for third-party tested, certified products is particularly high in this segment. This is not a race-to-the-bottom category. It's a premium wellness play that rewards trust and expertise — two things you have and Amazon doesn't.

Start your opening order conservatively: 2–3 brands, 3–4 SKUs per brand. Lead with your anxiety and senior joint SKUs. Create a simple "Hemp & CBD 101" shelf talker so customers can self-educate without monopolizing your staff's time.

The Compliance Checklist Before You Stock a Single Unit

Before any CBD product hits your shelf or product page:

  • ☐ Request a current Certificate of Analysis (COA) from the brand — confirms actual CBD content and verifies no detectable THC above legal limits or contaminants
  • ☐ Review every product description for drug claims — flag and remove any language that makes therapeutic promises
  • ☐ Train every customer-facing staff member on what they can say ("This is formulated to support calm behavior") vs. what they can't ("This treats anxiety")
  • ☐ Confirm your state's hemp retail requirements at your state agriculture department website — some states require retailer registration or specific label formats
  • ☐ Document your supplier relationship — who tested the product, when it was tested, and what the COA showed

This isn't bureaucracy. One staff member who can confidently walk a customer through the difference between full-spectrum and CBD isolate — and explain why that matters for their 14-year-old Labrador — closes more sales than a full end-cap display ever will.

Turn CBD Buyers Into Your Most Loyal Autoship Customers

They're not clicking around comparing prices. They've made a considered decision to invest in their pet's daily wellness. That relationship is worth protecting with a simple autoship offer.

Set up a 10–15% subscription discount on your top CBD SKUs (Recharge, Bold Subscriptions, or Stay AI all handle this natively on Shopify). The customer who finds a tincture that genuinely calms their dog during August thunderstorms is not switching to Amazon's generic hemp oil next month. They're coming back to the store that knew what to recommend, had the COAs on file, and made reordering effortless. That's not a customer Chewy can replicate. That's yours.

The Bottom Line

The pet CBD market is not a fad. It's a 32.5% CAGR category with built-in replenishment cadences, premium margins, and a buyer segment that values trust over price. The regulatory path is clear, the wholesale brands are established, and the compliance framework is straightforward. What's stopping most indie retailers isn't a real barrier — it's hesitation. And hesitation is exactly what the category leaders are counting on while they fill your local market.

Start with Pet Releaf and HolistaPet. Run a 90-day test. Track your reorder rate. By month four, you'll understand why this category should anchor your supplement section permanently.
