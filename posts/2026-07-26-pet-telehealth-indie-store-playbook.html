Pet Telehealth Just Hit $2.6 Billion. Chewy's Version Is Generic. Here's the Indie Store Playbook to Win It.

📅 July 26, 2026 ✍️ PetStore.com Editorial Team 🏷️ Strategy · Veterinary Health · Telehealth

On July 16, 2026, Zoetis — the largest animal health company on earth — announced it would acquire VitalRADS, a 24/7 veterinary teleradiology platform with AI-assisted diagnostics across 25+ species. The deal closes this quarter. This is not a niche tech story. It is a loud, expensive signal about where the $2.6 billion veterinary telehealth market is heading — and what smart independent pet stores should be doing about it right now.

The Signal That Changes Everything

The veterinary telehealth market was valued at $2.61 billion in 2026 and is growing at a 27.3% CAGR — roughly doubling every three years. Pet telemedicine specifically is on track to hit $7.45 billion by 2032. These aren't projections anchored in wishful thinking; they're driven by three real structural forces:

  • A vet shortage that isn't resolving — the AVMA projects a shortfall of 15,000 veterinarians by 2030. Same-day appointments are a luxury in most markets.
  • Pet humanization accelerating spend — owners who treat their pets as family members want the same on-demand health access they have for themselves.
  • Consumer comfort with telehealth after COVID — the mental barrier to virtual consultations is gone. Owners who Zoomed their own doctor in 2021 are ready to video-chat a vet in 2026.

Chewy recognized this early. Their "Connect with a Vet" service now offers video consultations inside the Chewy app. Walmart followed, partnering with Pawp to bundle 24/7 telehealth access through the Walmart Health ecosystem. The category is officially mainstream — which means the race to differentiate is on.

Why Chewy and Walmart's Version Is Actually Your Opportunity

Here's what Chewy and Walmart's telehealth offerings have in common: they're bolt-ons. Features, not identities. A checkbox on a slide deck that says "we offer virtual vet care." The experience is anonymous, volume-driven, and completely decoupled from product advice.

That's the gap. A telehealth visit on Chewy doesn't tell you which joint supplement is best for your Labrador's specific presentation. It doesn't connect your vet's recommendation to the right product in your cart. It doesn't follow up three weeks later to check if the probiotic protocol is working.

An independent pet store that bundles telehealth with genuine product expertise creates something the big platforms structurally cannot replicate: a complete pet health experience where the vet advice and the product purchase exist in the same trusted relationship. That's not a feature. That's a business model.

The Four Telehealth Platforms Worth Knowing

You don't need to hire veterinarians or build a platform. Several high-quality services offer white-label partnerships, affiliate revenue, or bundling-friendly models:

  • Dutch — Prescription-friendly telehealth for dogs and cats. Three membership tiers from $8.99/month (Essential) to $15.99/month (Premium) covering unlimited video consults plus Rx delivery. Their affiliate and co-marketing program is the most retail-friendly in the space.
  • Pawp — $24/month for unlimited 24/7 video chat plus a $3,000 emergency fund (one use per year, covers any vet ER bill). The emergency fund is a visceral selling point that closes hesitant buyers instantly.
  • AskVet — $29/month, focused on preventive coaching, nutrition guidance, and personalized care plans. Ideal for the wellness-forward customer who wants ongoing guidance, not just crisis response.
  • GuardianVets — Offers a white-label model aimed at pet retailers and veterinary practices, so your customers see your branding, not GuardianVets'. Contact them directly to discuss partnership terms.

The April 2026 Petwealth–Pawp integration is a useful reference model: every Petwealth health report now connects pet parents to a licensed vet on demand. That's the mechanic — connect a health data touchpoint to a live consultation. Your store can do the same thing starting with a product recommendation email.

How to Bundle Telehealth Without Owning a Veterinary Practice

The cleanest execution for an indie store is a Wellness Membership that stacks telehealth access on top of your existing autoship discount. Here's how the math works:

  • Charge members $19.99/month for the wellness membership
  • Include: 15% autoship discount on all orders + access to a bundled telehealth service (Dutch or Pawp at your wholesale rate)
  • Layer in: a monthly "vet pick" product (you curate a supplement or product your telehealth partner recommends, add it to their autoship at a discount)

At $19.99/month, you're covering the cost of the telehealth partnership and still netting $5–8 per member per month in pure membership fee — before they buy anything. And members with telehealth access buy more products, not fewer, because every consultation generates a product recommendation that leads them back to your store.

If setting up a full membership feels like too big a lift, start simpler: add a "virtual vet included" badge to your premium autoship tier and offer one free Dutch or AskVet consult as an onboarding benefit. Track redemptions. If more than 30% use it, you have proof of concept to build the full program.

The Product Stack That Complements Telehealth Perfectly

Telehealth consultations reliably generate demand in five product categories. Make sure you're well-stocked in all five:

  • Targeted supplements — joint support, digestive enzymes, omega-3s, calming adaptogens. A vet who recommends a probiotic will send the owner directly to your store if that's where they have a relationship.
  • Prescription-adjacent functional foods — limited-ingredient diets, hydrolyzed proteins, low-phosphorus senior formulas. These get recommended on telehealth calls constantly.
  • At-home health testing kits — microbiome tests, allergy panels, urinalysis strips. A telehealth vet who recommends a test and you stock it becomes a closed loop.
  • Monitoring tools — weight scales, smart feeders with intake tracking, GPS collars. Ongoing monitoring feeds data back into future telehealth visits.
  • Dental care products — vets recommend dental hygiene in roughly half of all consultations for dogs over age 3. Enzymatic toothpaste, water additives, dental chews.

Pricing Your Telehealth Bundle

The price anchoring here is favorable. A single in-office vet visit runs $80–$250 without diagnostics. A telehealth consult through Dutch or AskVet retails at $65–$75 per standalone appointment. Your bundle pricing should make the math feel obvious:

"For $19.99/month — less than the cost of one vet co-pay — you get unlimited virtual consultations, 15% off everything you already buy, and a monthly vet-approved product pick."

That's a message that writes itself. Test it in your next email campaign with a waitlist landing page before you build the full product. If you get 50 signups in the first week, you have your answer.

Marketing Telehealth: The Story That Sells Itself

The content angles for telehealth practically write themselves:

  • The 3am story — "Your dog ate something weird and it's midnight. Here's why members don't panic." Every pet owner has had this moment; this content converts like crazy on Facebook and Instagram.
  • The vet recommendation follow-through — "Our virtual vet recommended this supplement. Here's what it does and why we carry it." Pairs content with product perfectly.
  • The cost comparison post — "One emergency vet visit: $400. Our wellness membership for a year: $240. Plus you get all your food and supplements at 15% off." Numbers like this get shared.

For TikTok and Instagram Reels, show the actual app experience: pet owner sitting on the couch at night, quick video call with a vet, vet recommends a specific product, owner pulls it up on your store in the same session. That's a 30-second video that explains the entire value proposition without a single word of ad copy.

Your 30-Day Telehealth Launch Plan

You don't need to build this perfectly before launching. Here's the minimum viable version:

  1. Week 1 — Create a waitlist landing page for "PetStore.com Wellness Membership." Include the core benefit: virtual vet access + autoship savings. Drive traffic with one email to your list.
  2. Week 2 — Reach out to Dutch and AskVet about affiliate/partnership terms. Get pricing for bundled access. GuardianVets for a white-label conversation if you want your own branding.
  3. Week 3 — Stock or confirm stock on your five telehealth-adjacent product categories. Brief your customer service team on how telehealth works so they can answer questions confidently.
  4. Week 4 — Launch to your waitlist at a founding-member rate ($14.99/month versus the regular $19.99). Cap it at 100 founding members to create urgency. Send the results email: "X members joined in the first 48 hours — here's what they're getting."

Done well, your first 100 wellness members become the most powerful marketing asset you have: real customers who can speak to a real service that no mass retailer in your category is matching at the local, curated, relationship-driven level you can deliver.

The Bottom Line

Zoetis spending billions to acquire a veterinary telehealth platform is the same signal as Chewy building Autoship in 2014: the category is proven, the demand is real, and the companies that get there with a genuine value proposition early will own the relationship. Chewy and Walmart are offering telehealth as a commodity. You can offer it as the center of a complete pet health membership that makes customers' lives genuinely better.

The stores that bundle telehealth with expert product curation — not as an upsell, but as the core identity of what they are — won't need to compete on price with anyone. They'll be in a different business entirely.
